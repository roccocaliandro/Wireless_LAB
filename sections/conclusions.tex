\section{Conclusion}

The experiments confirmed that UDP gives the application full control over packet size. Furthermore, choosing $L = 1472$\,B avoids IP fragmentation but offers no protection against loss of data. So, on WiFi, datagram loss rates reached 32--78\,\% under heavy load because the sender transmits without flow or congestion control. Moreover, variability was higher on wireless links (standard deviation 40--78\,Mb/s vs.\ $<$1\,Mb/s on Ethernet), reflecting the shared-medium nature of 802.11. An important forward/reverse asymmetry appeared on WiFi, likely caused by hardware and driver differences between the two wireless adapters. Finally, TCP trades lower goodput for reliable and congestion-controlled delivery, whereas UDP remains preferable for loss-tolerant, real-time applications thanks to its lower per-packet overhead.
